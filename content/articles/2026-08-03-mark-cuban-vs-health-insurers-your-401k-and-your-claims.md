Payton Herres had a heart transplant as a child. For years her insurer covered the generic anti-rejection drug she takes every day. Then it stopped. She posted on Facebook, the post spread, and coverage was restored, at $1,000 per 90-day supply instead of the $180 she'd been paying. "Ghost approval," she called it: approved on paper, unaffordable in practice.

Mark Cuban saw the story and did two things. His pharmacy, Cost Plus Drugs, now supplies her medication for about $300. And he went after the insurers, and then after the rest of us. If you own shares in a fund that holds the biggest insurance carriers, he wrote, "you are part of the cost of health care problem in this country." He's naming names, UnitedHealth, Cigna, CVS, Elevance, Humana, Centene, and urging fund holders to tell their fund companies to divest or lose their business.

He isn't venting. He's built a business that undercuts the system he's describing, and he has the receipts. Americans spent $5.6 trillion on health care last year, and for a lot of patients the biggest cost arrives after the claim is denied.

## What's actually in your 401(k)

Cuban's factual claim is simply true. Vanguard's Total Stock Market fund holds all six of the insurers he names. So does every S&P 500 fund. If your retirement money is in a target-date fund, a balanced fund, or almost any broad index, you own them, and you've never once been asked.

That's the nature of owning the market: you own the parts you'd never pick. Oil companies, tobacco, defense contractors, and the insurer that denied your neighbor's physical therapy. Cuban's argument is that it's time to stop pretending that's neutral.

> **What to do:** Look up your largest fund's holdings on the fund company's website. Search for "UnitedHealth." Most retirees have never done this, and the answer is worth knowing whether or not you act on it.

## If you want to act on it

- **Ask, the way Cuban suggests.** Fund companies count complaints. A letter or a call to Vanguard, Fidelity, or BlackRock saying you want a broad fund without the major insurers is exactly the pressure he's organizing.
- **Switch to an ex-health-insurance or values-screened fund.** They exist, and some are cheap. Read the fee and the actual holdings; a few charge several times an index fund's cost and still hold companies you wouldn't like.
- **Use direct indexing** if your account is large enough. Some brokerages let you own the index as individual stocks and drop the ones you object to, with minimal drift from the market.
- **Buy your generics at Cost Plus Drugs** and pharmacies like it. For many generic prescriptions it beats a Medicare Part D copay outright, no insurance needed, and every dollar spent there is a dollar that doesn't pass through an insurer. This is Cuban's own tool, and it works today.

> **What to do:** Whatever you decide about the fund, check Cost Plus Drugs against your Part D copay for every generic you fill. Retirees regularly find the cash price is lower than their "covered" price.

## The fight that's yours no matter what

Herres got her drug back because strangers made noise and a billionaire noticed. Most people don't get a Mark Cuban. What they get is the appeals process, and insurers count on you not using it. Medicare Advantage plans deny millions of prior authorization requests a year; only a small share are appealed, and most of those appeals win.

**Original Medicare:** the denial shows on your Medicare Summary Notice. You have 120 days to request a redetermination, with four more levels behind it.

**Medicare Advantage:** 60 days from the notice to appeal. The plan must answer in 30 days for a service you haven't had, 60 for a payment, and 72 hours if your doctor says waiting could harm you. A second denial goes automatically to an independent reviewer.

**Part D drugs:** ask for a coverage determination and, with your doctor's support, an exception. A "ghost approval" at an unaffordable tier is what a tiering exception is for.

> **What to do:** Never accept the first no. Get the reason code from the provider's billing office, file before the deadline, and call your State Health Insurance Assistance Program at 1-877-839-2675 for free help with the paperwork.

Cuban's point is that you're not a bystander in this system. You're a shareholder in it, and a customer of it, and a patient inside it. The fund is one lever. The pharmacy counter is another. The appeal form is the third, and it's the one that puts money back in your own pocket first.
