Two different things are happening with Medicare drug coverage, and they're easy to mix up. Premiums for stand-alone Part D plans are likely to rise in 2027. The annual limit on what you pay out of pocket for covered drugs is not going anywhere. That cap is written into law, and for 2026 it is $2,100.

## How the cap works

Once your out-of-pocket spending on covered Part D drugs reaches $2,100 in a calendar year, you pay nothing more for covered drugs for the rest of that year. There is no more "donut hole." The coverage gap that used to leave people paying full price for months was eliminated when the cap took effect.

The number resets on January 1. It is also indexed, so expect it to move up a little each year. It was $2,000 in 2025 and $2,100 in 2026.

> **What to do:** Look at last year's pharmacy receipts or your plan's year-end summary. If you spent anywhere near $2,000 on covered drugs, the cap is doing real work for you, and it should factor into which plan you pick this fall.

## What counts toward it

- Your deductible, copays, and coinsurance for drugs covered by your plan.
- Payments made on your behalf by most manufacturer assistance programs and by Extra Help.

## What doesn't count

- **Premiums.** The monthly amount you pay for the plan itself is separate and never counts toward the cap.
- **Drugs your plan doesn't cover.** If a medication isn't on your plan's formulary, what you pay for it is outside the cap.
- **Part B drugs.** Medications given in a doctor's office or infusion center, such as many cancer drugs and injections, are billed under Part B and follow Part B cost rules.
- **Pharmacy purchases outside your plan**, such as paying cash with a discount card to get a lower price. Those dollars don't get reported to your plan.

> **What to do:** If you ever use a discount card instead of your insurance because the cash price is lower, ask the pharmacist whether the purchase can be submitted to your plan. If it can't, that money won't help you reach the cap.

## The payment option most people haven't used

The cap protects you from a runaway total, but it doesn't stop a single expensive prescription from landing all at once in January. That's what the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan is for. If you opt in, your plan bills you monthly for your out-of-pocket drug costs instead of collecting them at the pharmacy counter, and it spreads the balance across the remaining months of the year. There is no interest and no fee.

It's a budgeting tool, not a discount. You pay the same total either way. It helps most for people who fill one or two costly prescriptions early in the year and would rather see a predictable bill.

> **What to do:** Call your drug plan (the number is on your card) and ask to enroll in the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan. You can join at any time during the year, and you can leave it whenever you want.

## If the cap is still too high

Extra Help is the federal program that lowers drug costs for people with limited income and resources. In 2026 the income limits are roughly $23,000 for a single person and $31,000 for a married couple, with separate limits on savings. If you qualify, your copays drop to a few dollars per prescription and your premium may be covered. Apply through Social Security online or by phone; the application is free.
