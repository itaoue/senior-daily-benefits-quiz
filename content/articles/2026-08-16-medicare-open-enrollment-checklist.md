Every fall, from October 15 to December 7, you can switch Medicare Advantage or Part D drug plans for the coming year. If you do nothing, your current plan renews automatically. That sounds convenient. It's also how millions of people end up paying more than they need to, because plans change their prices, their drug lists, and their doctor networks every single year, and the notice explaining those changes arrives in a thick envelope most people never open.

Here are the five things to check. Have your Medicare card and your list of prescriptions next to you.

## 1. Read the "Annual Notice of Change" your plan mailed you

It arrives by September 30 and it's the only document that tells you what your plan is doing differently next year: new premium, new deductible, drugs moved to a pricier tier, doctors dropped from the network. Skim the summary table on the first pages. If your premium is going up more than a few dollars or a drug you take is no longer covered, that's your signal to shop.

## 2. Run your prescriptions through the Medicare Plan Finder

Go to Medicare.gov, choose "Find plans," and enter every drug you take with the dose and how often. The tool shows your total yearly cost for each plan in your area, premium and copays combined, and tells you which pharmacies are in network. This takes about 15 minutes and is the single highest-value thing on this list. People routinely find plans that cost several hundred dollars a year less for the exact same medications.

> **What to do:** Sort the results by "lowest yearly drug + premium cost," not by lowest premium. A $0-premium plan with high copays can cost far more over a year than a $30-a-month plan that covers your drugs well.

## 3. Check whether you qualify for Extra Help

Extra Help is a federal program that pays most of your Part D premium and caps your copays at a few dollars per prescription. Since 2024 the program has a single full-benefit level, and the income limit is higher than most people assume, roughly $23,000 a year for a single person and $31,000 for a couple, with separate limits on savings. Many people who were turned down years ago now qualify. You can apply online at SSA.gov in about ten minutes.

## 4. Check whether a Medicare Savings Program will pay your Part B premium

The Part B premium comes out of your Social Security check before you ever see it. Medicare Savings Programs, run by your state, pay that premium for people with limited income. The most common one, QMB, also covers your Medicare deductibles and copays. Income limits vary by state and several states have raised or removed the asset test. Call your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) and ask them to screen you. It's free.

## 5. If you're on Medicare Advantage, confirm your doctors are still in network

Networks change on January 1. Call the office of every doctor and specialist you see and ask, "Will you be in network for [plan name] in 2027?" Don't rely on the plan's online directory, which is often out of date.

> **What to do:** If you switch from Medicare Advantage back to Original Medicare during Open Enrollment, you may not be guaranteed a Medigap policy at standard rates unless you're within your first year on Advantage or your state has special rules. Ask SHIP before you make that move.

## One more thing

There's a hard cap on what you pay out of pocket for Part D drugs each year. It was $2,000 in 2025 and rises slightly with inflation. If you take expensive medications, that cap matters more than the premium, and every plan has to honor it.
