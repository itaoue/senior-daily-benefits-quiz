An overpayment notice is one of the most alarming letters Social Security sends. It says the agency paid you more than it should have, often for years, and it wants the money back. Sometimes the amount is a few hundred dollars. Sometimes it's tens of thousands.

Here is what changed in 2025: for new overpayments, the default recovery rate for retirement and disability benefits is now **50% of your monthly check** until the balance is repaid. For years it was 10%. If you don't respond, the withholding starts automatically 90 days after the notice date. Filing a waiver, appeal, or rate-change request within 30 days pauses collection while it is reviewed.

You have three separate tools, and you can use more than one at the same time.

## Tool 1: Ask for a lower repayment rate (Form SSA-634)

This is the fastest fix. Social Security will reduce the withholding to an amount you can afford, in many cases as little as $10 a month, if you show that 50% would leave you unable to cover basic expenses. The form asks for your monthly income and bills. You don't have to prove the overpayment was their fault.

> **What to do:** Call 1-800-772-1213 or your local office and ask for a change in the recovery rate, or file SSA-634 online. Do this first, before anything else, because it stops the 50% withholding while your other requests are being reviewed.

## Tool 2: Ask for a waiver (Form SSA-632)

A waiver means you don't have to repay at all. You qualify if two things are true: the overpayment wasn't your fault, and repaying would cause hardship or would be unfair. "Not your fault" includes cases where you reported a change and the agency didn't act on it, or where you had no reasonable way to know you were being overpaid. Overpayments under $2,000 can often be waived with a phone call and no paperwork.

Requesting a waiver stops collection while it's under review. If the request is denied, you can ask for a personal conference and then a formal appeal.

## Tool 3: Dispute the amount (Form SSA-561)

If you think the agency's math is wrong, or you weren't overpaid at all, file a Request for Reconsideration within **60 days** of the notice date. Ask your local office for the file that shows how they calculated the amount. Errors are common, especially in cases involving work earnings, pensions, or a spouse's benefits.

> **What to do:** Filing within 30 days of the notice keeps benefits flowing at the full rate while the appeal is decided. After 30 days you can still appeal, but withholding may begin in the meantime.

## What not to do

Don't ignore the letter. Don't put the repayment on a credit card without first trying the three tools above. And don't pay anyone who calls you claiming to be from Social Security and offering to "settle" the overpayment for a fee. The agency never calls to demand payment, never asks for gift cards or wire transfers, and never threatens arrest.

If the amount is large and you're getting nowhere, your local Legal Aid office or Area Agency on Aging can often help for free.
