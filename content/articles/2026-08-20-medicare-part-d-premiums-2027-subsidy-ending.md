When the $2,000 out-of-pocket cap took effect in 2025, insurers were suddenly on the hook for far more of the cost of expensive drugs. To keep them from passing that straight into premiums, Medicare ran a temporary "premium stabilization" program that paid stand-alone drug plans extra and limited how much any plan's premium could rise from one year to the next. It cost about $9.8 billion over 2025 and 2026.

That program ends December 31. For 2027, plans set premiums on their own.

## Who is affected

- **Stand-alone Part D plans** (the ones people with Original Medicare buy separately): yes. Roughly 25 million people.
- **Medicare Advantage plans with drug coverage:** mostly no. The stabilization program was never applied to them, so they aren't losing anything.
- **People with Extra Help:** partly protected. The program pays most or all of the premium for a benchmark plan, but the list of benchmark plans can change.

## What Medicare expects

CMS's own projection, published with the 2027 bid figures, is that about 75% of stand-alone plan enrollees will see a premium increase. Around 30% will see increases under $10 a month. Around 45% will see increases mostly in the $11 to $20 range. A smaller group will see more, and some plans will leave certain regions entirely.

The out-of-pocket cap on drug costs is not affected. That's law, and it rises with inflation.

> **What to do:** Don't assume your current plan is still the right one. In a year when every insurer is repricing, the cheapest plan for your prescriptions in 2026 may not be the cheapest in 2027.

## The timeline

- **By September 30:** your plan mails an Annual Notice of Change (ANOC). It shows next year's premium, deductible, copays, and any changes to which drugs are covered and which pharmacies are preferred. Read it. This is the document that tells you if your plan just got worse.
- **October 1:** 2027 plan details go live on Medicare's Plan Finder.
- **October 15 to December 7:** Open Enrollment. Any change you make takes effect January 1.
- **January 1 to March 31:** Medicare Advantage members only can switch once more. Stand-alone Part D members cannot.

> **What to do:** When the ANOC arrives, check three things in this order: is each of your drugs still covered, did any move to a higher tier, and is your pharmacy still "preferred." A $5 premium increase is minor. A drug moving from tier 2 to tier 4 is not.

## How to compare plans in 20 minutes

Go to medicare.gov/plan-compare, enter your ZIP code and your drugs with exact doses, and pick your pharmacy. The tool ranks plans by **total estimated yearly cost**, which is premium plus what you'd pay at the counter for your actual prescriptions. That's the number to compare, not the premium alone. A plan with a $0 premium and high copays on your one expensive drug can cost far more than a $40 plan that covers it well.

If you'd rather talk to a person, your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) does this comparison for free by phone or in person. Call 1-877-839-2675.

## Ways to lower the bill regardless of plan

- **Extra Help.** Income under roughly $23,000 (single) or $31,000 (couple) with limited savings. It cuts premiums and copays sharply. Apply through Social Security.
- **State Pharmaceutical Assistance Programs.** About two dozen states have their own. Plan Finder lists them.
- **Generics and 90-day mail order**, which most plans price lower than 30-day retail.
- **The Medicare Prescription Payment Plan**, which doesn't lower costs but spreads them into monthly bills.
