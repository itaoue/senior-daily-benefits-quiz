Senior discounts are the strangest kind of benefit: worth real money, offered by thousands of businesses, and almost never mentioned unless you bring them up. Retailers don't put them on signs because they don't want to give a discount to every customer who qualifies, only to the ones who ask.

So ask. The sentence is: **"Do you offer a senior discount?"** Say it every time you pay, everywhere. The worst answer you'll get is no.

Here's what we've confirmed. Rules change by location, so treat this as a starting list and always ask.

## Retail, by day of the week

- **Tuesday:** Ross Dress for Less takes 10% off for shoppers 55 and up.
- **Wednesday:** Kohl's takes 15% off in store for customers 60 and over. Walgreens runs "Senior Day" specials for myWalgreens members 55+ on select dates.
- **First Wednesday of the month:** Rite Aid's Wellness65+ members get 10% off most items, in some stores 20%.
- **Any day:** Michaels gives 10% off to shoppers 55+ with a free Rewards account. Goodwill stores in most regions have a weekly senior day, usually 20–25% off.

## Groceries and pharmacies

Most regional grocery chains have a senior day; Fred Meyer, Harris Teeter, and many Kroger banners run one, typically 5% on the first Tuesday or Wednesday. Pharmacies inside these stores often extend the discount to over-the-counter items but not prescriptions. Ask at the customer service desk, not the register; the cashiers frequently don't know.

## Travel

- **Amtrak:** 10% off most fares for travelers 65 and older.
- **Airlines:** United, Delta, and American offer senior fares on some routes, but only by phone. It's worth one call before booking online.
- **Hotels:** Most major chains take 10–15% off for AARP members or guests over 62; Hyatt's senior rate can run up to 50% off at some properties.
- **National parks:** The lifetime Senior Pass costs $80 for U.S. citizens 62 and older and covers entrance for everyone in your car at every national park and federal recreation site.
- **Car rentals:** Avis, Budget, and Hertz give AARP members a discount plus a free additional driver.

## Restaurants and entertainment

Denny's, IHOP, and many Applebee's locations have a senior menu or 10–15% off for 55+. Regal and AMC theaters sell senior tickets (60+) at every showing, not just matinees. Local museums, zoos, and community theaters almost always have a senior rate that isn't on the website.

## Phone and internet

This one isn't a discount, it's a federal program: **Lifeline** takes up to $9.25 a month off phone or internet service for people on Medicaid, SNAP, SSI, or with income under 135% of the poverty line. Apply at LifelineSupport.org. Separately, T-Mobile and Verizon sell 55+ plans that are cheaper than their standard plans for the same service.

> **What to do this week:** Write "senior discount?" on a sticky note and put it on your wallet. It sounds silly. It also works, and readers tell us it's worth $30–$60 a month within the first few weeks.

## Is AARP worth it?

For most people over 50, yes. Membership is $15 for the first year and the hotel, rental car, and restaurant discounts alone usually pay for it several times over. If you never travel and rarely eat out, you can skip it and still get most of the retail discounts above with nothing more than an ID.
