Every year some Medicare Advantage plans decide certain counties are no longer profitable and pull out. When that happens you get a letter, usually in October, saying your plan won't be offered next year. It's easy to panic. Don't. This is one of the few situations where the rules are actually on your side.

## What you get when your plan leaves

**A Special Enrollment Period.** You can join a different Medicare Advantage plan or return to Original Medicare from December 8 through the end of February, on top of the normal Open Enrollment window. If you do nothing, you'll be moved to Original Medicare on January 1.

**A guaranteed right to buy Medigap.** This is the big one. Normally, if you've been on Medicare Advantage for more than a year and want to switch to Original Medicare plus a Medigap supplement, insurers in most states can ask health questions and turn you down or charge more. When your plan leaves your area, that rule is suspended: you have **63 days** from the date your coverage ends to buy certain Medigap plans (A, B, D, G, K, or L) at the standard rate, with no health questions.

For anyone with a chronic condition who has felt trapped in Advantage, this is the door.

## How to decide

Ask yourself three questions.

- **Are my doctors in another Advantage plan's network?** Call each office and ask by plan name. If yes, and the drug coverage checks out on the Medicare Plan Finder, switching to another Advantage plan is the simplest path.
- **Do I travel or split time between states?** Original Medicare works with any provider that accepts Medicare, anywhere in the country. Advantage plans generally don't.
- **Can I afford a Medigap premium?** Plan G runs roughly $130 to $250 a month depending on your age and state. In exchange, you'll have almost no out-of-pocket costs for covered care. If that premium isn't manageable, an Advantage plan with a low premium and a reasonable out-of-pocket maximum may be the better fit.

> **What to do:** Call your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) the week the letter arrives. They're free, they're not selling anything, and they'll pull up every plan in your county. Don't sign anything from an agent who calls you first.

## Don't forget the drug plan

If you move to Original Medicare, you also need a standalone Part D drug plan; Medigap doesn't include one. Enroll during the same window so there's no gap. If you go more than 63 days without drug coverage, a permanent late penalty gets added to your premium.

## Two traps

Some agents will steer you toward whichever Advantage plan pays them the most. Ask them directly: "Will you show me every plan in my county, including ones you don't sell?" And don't let the 63-day Medigap window close while you're deciding; if you miss it, the health questions come back.
