Nearly nine in ten people over 65 say they want to stay in their own home as they age. The reason most eventually don't isn't preference. It's that a fall, a set of stairs, or a bathtub makes the house unworkable, and nobody planned for it.

The numbers favor planning ahead.

## What each option costs

**Assisted living:** the national median is roughly $5,500 to $6,000 a month, and considerably more in coastal metros. That's $65,000 to $75,000 a year, paid mostly out of pocket; Medicare does not cover it.

**A home health aide** for 20 hours a week, the level many people need for a few years before anything more intensive, runs about $2,500 to $3,000 a month.

**Making a house safe to age in** is a one-time cost: a walk-in shower or tub conversion ($3,000 to $8,000), grab bars and a comfort-height toilet ($300 to $800), a stair lift ($3,000 to $5,000), lever handles, better lighting, and a first-floor bedroom if the house allows it. Most complete packages land between $10,000 and $20,000. That's two to four months of assisted living, once.

## The programs that pay for staying home

Most people don't know these exist. All are free to apply for.

- **Area Agency on Aging.** Every county has one. They run home-delivered meals, transportation, respite care, and in many areas a home modification or minor repair program for seniors. Find yours at eldercare.acl.gov or by calling 1-800-677-1116.
- **Medicaid Home and Community Based Services waivers.** If your income and assets are low enough to qualify for Medicaid, every state has a waiver program that pays for in-home care, and many pay for modifications like ramps and bathroom conversions, specifically because it's cheaper than a nursing home.
- **USDA Section 504 Home Repair grants.** For homeowners 62 and older in rural areas with low income, grants of up to $10,000 (loans up to $40,000 at 1%) to remove health and safety hazards. Bathroom conversions and stair lifts qualify.
- **Weatherization Assistance Program.** Free insulation, air sealing, and sometimes a furnace or water heater for income-eligible households. Seniors get priority. Lower heating bills are a permanent raise on a fixed income.
- **Veterans:** the VA's HISA grant pays up to $6,800 for home modifications for veterans with a service-connected disability, $2,000 for non-service-connected, and the Aid & Attendance benefit adds a monthly amount for veterans or surviving spouses who need help at home.
- **Property tax relief** for seniors, in most states, so that the house itself stays affordable.

> **What to do this month:** Call your Area Agency on Aging and ask two questions: "Do you have a home modification program?" and "Can someone do a home safety assessment?" Many will send an occupational therapist to walk through the house for free and tell you exactly what to fix first. Then get one quote for the bathroom, which is where most falls happen.

## What technology can and can't do

Medical alert pendants ($25 to $45 a month), video doorbells, stove auto-shutoffs, and medication dispensers all help, and a monitored security system doubles as a way for family to check in. None of them replace a grab bar. Fix the physical house first, then add the gadgets.

Staying home isn't right for everyone, and a good assisted living community can be the better choice when isolation or complex medical needs are the issue. But it should be a choice, made early, with the numbers in front of you, not a decision forced by a fall.
