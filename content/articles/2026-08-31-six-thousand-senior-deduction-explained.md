Tucked into the 2025 tax law was a deduction aimed squarely at retirees, and it's more generous than the headlines suggested. Here's how it works.

## The basics

- **Amount:** $6,000 per person who is 65 or older by the end of the tax year. A married couple where both spouses are 65+ gets $12,000.
- **Years:** tax years 2025 through 2028. The first return it appears on is the 2025 return you file in early 2026 (or the one you'll file in spring 2027 for 2026).
- **You don't have to itemize.** It's available on top of the standard deduction and on top of the existing extra standard deduction for people 65+. It also applies if you do itemize.
- **Phase-out:** the deduction shrinks by 6 cents for every dollar of modified adjusted gross income above **$75,000** (single) or **$150,000** (married filing jointly). It disappears entirely at $175,000 single / $250,000 joint.
- **Requirements:** you need a valid Social Security number on the return, and married couples must file jointly to claim it.

## What it's worth

For a single retiree in the 12% bracket, $6,000 off taxable income is about $720 in tax saved. For a couple in the 22% bracket, $12,000 off is about $2,640. It doesn't reduce tax on Social Security benefits directly, but by lowering taxable income it can pull some people below the thresholds where benefits become taxable, which is a second, quieter saving.

## The move to make before December 31

Because the deduction phases out based on income, the timing of income matters. Two examples:

- **Roth conversions.** If you're converting part of a traditional IRA to a Roth each year, size the conversion so your income stays under the $75,000 / $150,000 line. Going over doesn't just add tax on the conversion; it also claws back part of this deduction.
- **Required minimum distributions and capital gains.** If you have flexibility about when to sell an investment or take a distribution, spreading it across two tax years can keep both years under the phase-out.

> **What to do:** Pull last year's return and find your adjusted gross income on the first page. If it's within $20,000 of the threshold for your filing status, ask your tax preparer whether any income can be shifted before year-end. If you do your own taxes, the software will calculate the deduction automatically, but it can't move income for you.

## Two things it is not

It is not a repeal of taxes on Social Security benefits, despite how some campaign language described it. Benefits are taxed the same way they were; the deduction just lowers your overall taxable income.

And it is not permanent. Unless Congress extends it, it ends after the 2028 tax year. Plan on it for four returns and treat anything beyond that as a bonus.
