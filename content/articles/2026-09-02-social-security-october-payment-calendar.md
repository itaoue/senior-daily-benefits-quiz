Social Security pays on a fixed calendar, and the calendar has a rule that trips people up a few times a year: when a payment date falls on a weekend or federal holiday, the money arrives the business day **before**. Usually that's a minor shift. In October 2026 it means one group of people gets paid twice in the same month.

## October 2026, by group

**Supplemental Security Income (SSI)** normally pays on the 1st.
- Thursday, **October 1**: the October payment.
- Friday, **October 30**: the *November* payment, moved up because November 1 is a Sunday.
- There is no SSI deposit in November. December's arrives on December 1.

**People who receive both SSI and Social Security, or who started Social Security before May 1997**, are paid on the 3rd.
- Friday, **October 2**: moved up because October 3 is a Saturday.

**Everyone else on retirement, survivor, or disability benefits** is paid on a Wednesday based on birthday:
- Birthday on the 1st through 10th: Wednesday, **October 14**
- Birthday on the 11th through 20th: Wednesday, **October 21**
- Birthday on the 21st through 31st: Wednesday, **October 28**

## Why the double payment matters

The October 30 deposit is your November money. If you spend it as a bonus, November will be very long. If you budget by the month, treat that deposit as if it arrived on November 1.

> **What to do:** Set up an automatic transfer of that second deposit into a separate savings account the day it lands, then move it back on November 1. Or simply write "NOV" on the calendar next to October 30. Small thing; it prevents a real problem.

## Other calendar notes for the rest of 2026

- The **cost-of-living adjustment (COLA)** for 2027 will be announced in mid-October, after the September inflation numbers come out. It takes effect with the January 2027 payments, and the new Medicare Part B premium, which is deducted from your check, is announced around the same time.
- The **2027 Medicare premium** deduction can eat part or all of a small COLA. If your net check goes down in January, that's usually why, and Medicare Savings Programs can help if your income is limited.
- **Paper checks** are being phased out in favor of direct deposit and the Direct Express debit card. If you still get a paper check, switching now avoids delays.

## If a payment is late

Wait three business days after the scheduled date, then call 1-800-772-1213 or contact your bank. Don't respond to any call, text, or email claiming your payment is "suspended" and asking you to confirm information. Social Security doesn't suspend benefits by phone, and it never asks for your number to release a payment.
